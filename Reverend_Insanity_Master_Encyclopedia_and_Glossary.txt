I. SYSTEM TRANSLATION RULES & STYLE GUIDELINES
- Natural Literary Localization: Translate into immersive, publication-grade English dark fantasy prose matching Gu Zhen Ren's raw philosophical, ruthless, and cosmic nihilism.
- Spoken Dialogue Cadence: Perfectly preserve character demeanor—the indifferent, calculating pragmatism of Fang Yuan, the haughtiness of Gu Immortals, the grief/rage of righteous clan elders, the playful arrogance of Bai Ning Bing, and the affectionate cries of young juniors like Yao Le (Lele).
- Zero Censorship & Zero Truncation: NEVER skip, bridge, summarize, or censor graphic dark fantasy scenes, combat, beast feeding, torture, or gore. Translate 100% of the text with complete fidelity.
- Pinyin Capitalization: Strictly maintain proper Pinyin capitalization for Chinese names (Fang Yuan, Gu Yue Fang Yuan, Bai Ning Bing, Feng Jiu Ge, Hei Lou Lan, Duke Long, Star Constellation).
- Chinese Idioms: Translate idioms according to their actual literary meaning in context (e.g. 鸡犬不留 -> "leaving none alive, not even the chickens and dogs"; 人生地不熟 -> "a stranger in an unfamiliar place"). Avoid robotic word-for-word machine translation.
- Zero Meta-Artifacts: NEVER include translator notes, editorial remarks, or parenthetical omissions. Output ONLY pure translated chapter text.

- Classical Poetry, Chanted Songs & Mythic Parables: When characters recite classical Chinese poems, sing martial ballads (e.g. Feng Jiu Ge's Destiny/Submission/Separation Song, Fang Yuan's verses), or when narrating excerpts from <<The Legends of Ren Zu>> (人祖传):
  * Direct Emotional Weight & Punchy Power: Translate poems into evocative, rhythmically resonant English verse prioritizing direct emotional weight, heroic clarity, and poetic meter rather than overly literal obscure nature metaphors (e.g. render "早岁已知世事艰...千磨万击心铸铁...炼蛊炼人还炼天" as "In my youth I knew the hardships of the world... A heart of steel forged from countless setbacks... Refine Gu! Refine Human! Refine Heaven!!").
  * Exclamatory Battle-Cry Climaxes: Use sharp, punchy imperative and exclamatory phrasing for climactic poem endings rather than weak passive/participle forms.
  * In <<The Legends of Ren Zu>> and mythic parables, preserve strict anaphoric repetition (e.g. repeating "On what basis... / If such... / Then let me...") and word-for-word identical refrains across allegorical birds, beasts, and fishes to maintain the sacred, liturgical fable cadence.
